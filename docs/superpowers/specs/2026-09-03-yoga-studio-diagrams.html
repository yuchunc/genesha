  • The model explains her two different 2-class prices. 素容 paid 900 for 2堂 = 450×2, the drop-in rate. 彩華 paid 800 for 2堂 = 400×2, the package rate. As a flat price integer both were arbitrary numbers; as a package plus a session count they are structurally different sales, and which one she used becomes visible.
  • custom_amount instead of a discount field. Her doc records final agreed numbers — 400, 800, 900, 1200 — never deltas. She types what was agreed; the system never asks her to compute a difference. Adjustments can also go up, and "how much did I comp" stays derivable as list_price − payable.
  • 按摩器代購 is custom_amount = 0 plus a note, not a null amount and not a fake number. payment.amount is NOT NULL — no nullable money in the schema.
  • No month column anywhere. A purchase's period is derived from the dates of its attendance rows. Cost: monthly reporting joins through attendance → session, and a purchase with no attendance rows has no period. Benefit: nothing to drift, and month-spanning purchases need no special case.
  • purchase_id is Payment's only foreign key. Because a drop-in is also a purchase, payment needs no nullable FK pair and no polymorphic association. Student is reached via the purchase.
  • attendance.purchase_id is nullable, and that is the point. A makeup has no sale behind it — it is paid for by a credit. 允一's 無費用，補颱風假 row is exactly this shape.
  • 調課 and 補課 share one mechanism. Both are kind: :makeup consuming a credit; 宜群's 調8/21 and 蘭子's 補7/10颱風假 differ only in credit.source.

Guard on consumption — all three must hold, enforced in code:
1 · same student  ·  2 · consumed_by IS NULL  ·  3 · expires_on IS NULL OR ≥ session.date
Cancellation must be idempotent — re-running it may never mint duplicate credits.
  • Portability is real, not theoretical. 蘭子 earned a Friday cancellation credit and could spend it on Monday 8/17 or 8/31 — so credits are held by the student, never by the slot.
  • Why package credits expire but cancellation credits don't. 宜群 swapped 8/21→8/17 inside August, while July's 颱風假 credits were still being spent in August. The asymmetry is her actual behaviour.
  • The count is configuration, not a constant. included_makeups lives on the package: 1 for 月課程, 0 for 單堂 and 體驗. Changing the policy later is a data edit, not a migration.
  • Expiry is minted at session assignment, not purchase creation. A purchase has no month of its own, so expiry is the last day of the calendar month of its earliest session, in Asia/Taipei.

帳後五碼 is a lookup hint for a human, not a receipt — 5 digits prove nothing about amount, date, or sender.
So no unique constraint on reported_last5; instead flag repeats as a likely double-post or copy-paste error.
  • Why list_price is stored even when overridden. It is the snapshot of what the package cost at sale time, so the price list can change without rewriting history — and it is what makes the override visible rather than silent.
  • custom_amount is freeform. Any integer, above or below list, zero included. No percentage rule, no rounding policy, no discount arithmetic to keep consistent.
  • Manual entry may go straight to :confirmed — she is the human, and for cash she already knows. The invariant is that a human action is required, not that a claim step happened.
  • Outstanding = Σ payable − Σ confirmed payments. Unconfirmed claims are deliberately excluded, so "who still owes me" never counts money she hasn't actually seen.
  • One transfer may become several payment rows. She places the amounts herself, so payment.amount is the portion applied to that purchase, not the bank transaction total — and split rows legitimately share a reported_last5. Duplicate detection must therefore flag a repeat only across different students, or two rows against the same purchase; same student, same date, distinct purchases is a split, not an error.
  • A future :ecpay source could self-confirm. A webhook-verified payment is machine-provable, so one ledger holds two source types at two trust levels — LINE claims stay human-confirmed.
